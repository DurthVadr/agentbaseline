Five signals to measure

{{/* The paper's "Measuring the Factory" section, in plain sentences. */}}

Reaching a higher maturity level does not prove that a factory is secure. What proves it is what happens when something goes wrong: how fast a problem is found, and how far it spreads. The paper names five signals of a secure factory, to measure in place of simple counts.

{{ partial "software-factory-signals.html" . }} {{- if $sf.showMaturity }}

Three maturity levels

{{/* What a level is, then the paper's caveat, which points back at the signals. */}}

A factory runs the same five stages at every level. What changes is where it runs, who coordinates the agents, and which controls guard each stage. Each level builds on the one below, and a team picks the level that fits it. A level describes how a factory is built, not how secure it is. The five signals measure that.

{{ partial "software-factory-levels.html" . }} {{- end }}

Read the paper

{{/* Why to read the paper, not a list of its contents: it starts from what went wrong (PocketOS, d3 156; Hugging Face, d3 176) and builds the factory that guards against it. */}}

The paper explains why each control is there. It starts from real incidents, such as an agent that deleted a database in nine seconds, and another that escaped its sandbox and tried to hide its tracks. Then it shows how to build a factory that guards against them, one level at a time.
